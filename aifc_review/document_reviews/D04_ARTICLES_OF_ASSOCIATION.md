## 1. What this document actually is

**FINDING TYPE: FACT.** This is **not** a bespoke drafting exercise. It is the **AIFC Standard
Articles of Association for Private Companies (Schedule 5 to the AIFC Companies Rules)**, adopted
close to verbatim, with a small number of company-specific substitutions.

The evidence is direct textual identity in the most distinctive passages:

| Draft | Reported Schedule 5 wording |
|---|---|
| line 358: *"The Company must have at least 1 Director, who is a natural person and has an Individual Identification Number."* | Identical **[T1-snippet]** |
| line 33: *"Chief Executive Officer means the chief executive officer of the Company, who is a natural person and has an Individual Identification Number, appointed by the Shareholders or Directors."* | Identical **[T1-snippet]** |
| line 367: *"...who shall be a natural person and a resident of the Republic of Kazakhstan. The Chief Executive Officer shall have the broadest powers to act in all circumstances in the name of the Company, within the limits of the corporate objects and subject to powers expressly reserved by law for Shareholders' meetings and the Directors."* | Identical **[T1-snippet]** |

**This reframes the entire review, in three ways.**

1. **The most alarming clauses are not the drafter's inventions.** The IIN requirement and the
   CEO-residency requirement are the AIFC's own model wording. Criticising them as drafting errors
   would be wrong.
2. **But the drafter has already departed from the model** — in the company name [line 99], the
   registered office [line 107], the objects [line 114], the share capital [line 128], the language
   clause [lines 590–591] and the signature block [lines 594–607]. The Articles are therefore
   **not** "the Standard Articles adopted in their entirety".
3. **Which means Wiese Advisory is free to depart further.** Because the entirety-adoption route
   has already been abandoned, the residency and IIN clauses can in principle be removed. That is
   the key that unlocks the CEO blocker (§5.3).

**The consequential filing requirement — and it is missing from the package:**

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL REQUIREMENT [T1-snippet].** Where the Standard Articles are not adopted in
their entirety, the company must submit to the Registrar, **before the Articles are adopted**, a
**statement by the Incorporators** that the proposed Articles comply with the Companies
Regulations, the Rules and all other applicable AIFC Regulations and Rules. Further, proposed
Articles that do not adopt the Standard Articles in their entirety **must include provision for
specified matters set out in the Rules**.

Neither the statement nor a check against that list of specified matters exists in the package.

**Action:** obtain the Companies Rules provision enumerating the required matters, check the draft
against it item by item, and prepare the incorporators' compliance statement. Until that is done,
whether these Articles are filable is `UNCONFIRMED`.

## 2. The numbering defect — the English text has no article numbers at all

**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: FACT · Evidence: whole document; specifically lines 148–163 vs 165–177, and line 405**

The **Russian** mirror is numbered throughout: `8.1`–`8.5` [lines 165–177], `9.1`–`9.7` [193–199],
`10.1`–`10.3`, `11.1`–`11.3`, `13.1`–`13.2`, `14.1`–`14.5`, `15.1`–`15.12`, `16.1`–`16.8`,
`18.1`–`18.3`, `20.1`–`20.3`, `21.1`–`21.4`, `24.1`–`24.15`, `27.1`–`27.5`, `29.1`–`29.8`.

The **English** text carries **no numbers whatsoever** — only unnumbered paragraphs under
capitalised headings.

Two consequences, the second serious:

1. Nothing in the English can be cited. A registrar's comment, a bank's query, an amendment
   resolution or a court submission cannot refer to "Article 18.2"; it must quote the text.
2. **The English text cross-refers to numbers that exist only in the Russian.** Line 405:

   > "For the purposes of **the section 21.3.** where 2 or more Shareholders die..."

   There is no "21.3" anywhere in the English text. The cross-reference is a dangling pointer,
   resolvable only by reading the Russian. Since the language clause declares that **English
   prevails** [line 591], the prevailing text contains an unresolvable internal reference.

**Recommended treatment:** number every article and sub-article in **both** languages, identically,
and re-point all cross-references. This is mechanical and should be done before anything else,
because every other recommendation in this report needs a citable anchor.

## 5. The six focus areas

### 5.1 CEO definition [line 33] — requires an IIN
**Severity: BLOCKER (in combination with the Resolution) · FINDING TYPE: FACT + UNCONFIRMED**

> "**Chief Executive Officer** means the chief executive officer of the Company, who is a natural
> person and **has an Individual Identification Number**, appointed by the Shareholders or
> Directors."

This is verbatim Schedule 5 **[T1-snippet]**. Alexander Wiese has no IIN. The Resolution appoints
him CEO [Res. line 21]. **The definition therefore excludes the person appointed.**

Two independent disqualifications operate on the same appointment: the IIN requirement here, and
the residency requirement at line 367 (§5.3). Curing one does not cure the other.

**Recommended treatment: delete the definition** along with the CEO office (§5.3). If a CEO is
retained, the appointee must hold an IIN **and** be Kazakhstan-resident — i.e. must be someone
other than Alexander Wiese.

### 5.2 NUMBER OF DIRECTORS [line 358] — requires an IIN
**Severity: BLOCKER (pending confirmation) · FINDING TYPE: UNCONFIRMED**

> "The Company must have at least 1 Director, who is a natural person and **has an Individual
> Identification Number**."

Verbatim Schedule 5 **[T1-snippet]**. This is **the most consequential open question in the entire
review**, because unlike the CEO office — which can simply be abolished — the company **must** have
a director, and the Resolution appoints Alexander Wiese to that role [Res. line 23].

Three possible positions, with very different consequences:

| Position | Consequence |
|---|---|
| (a) The IIN requirement is **model-form wording only**, and bespoke Articles may omit it | Delete the words. Alexander Wiese can be appointed Director. **No delay.** |
| (b) The requirement is **Regulations- or Rules-level** and binds every AIFC company | Alexander Wiese **must obtain a Kazakhstan IIN before he can be appointed**. This becomes the **critical path** for the whole incorporation. |
| (c) The requirement binds, but can be satisfied by **any one** director holding an IIN (as the public-company Standard Articles are reported to provide: *"at all times shall have at least 1 director who has an Individual Identification Number"* **[T1-snippet]**) | A second, IIN-holding director would be needed — an outcome that puts a third party on the board of a company the mandate wants kept simple |

Secondary commentary supports the view that a foreign national appointed director **must obtain an
IIN**, applied for through the Expat Centre, and that there is **no residency requirement** for
directors **[Tier 3 — commentary, must not override primary law]**.

**Recommended treatment:**
1. Put the question to AFSA / Kazakh counsel **in writing**, distinguishing model-form from binding
   requirement.
2. **In parallel, begin the IIN application for Alexander Wiese now.** It is likely to be needed on
   any of positions (b) or (c), it has a lead time, and it is the item most capable of delaying the
   filing. Do not wait for the legal answer to start it.
3. If the answer is (a), delete the IIN words from [358] as an unnecessary restriction.

### 5.3 POWERS OF DIRECTORS [lines 365–367] — the CEO clause
**Severity: BLOCKER · FINDING TYPE: FACT + LEGAL INTERPRETATION**

> [365] "the business of the Company must be managed by the Directors **or** by another natural
> person appointed by the Shareholders or Directors and bearing the title of Chief Executive
> Officer."
>
> [367] "The Shareholders or Directors **may** appoint ... the Chief Executive Officer, who shall
> be a natural person and **a resident of the Republic of Kazakhstan**. The Chief Executive Officer
> shall have **the broadest powers to act in all circumstances in the name of the Company**, within
> the limits of the corporate objects and subject to powers expressly reserved by law for
> Shareholders' meetings and the Directors. He shall represent the Company in its dealings with
> third parties."

**Three findings.**

**(a) The package appoints a CEO its own Articles disqualify.** Alexander Wiese is resident in
Switzerland [established fact]. Line 367 requires Kazakhstan residency. The Resolution appoints him
CEO [Res. line 21]. The appointment is void under the instrument adopted alongside it on the same
day. **BLOCKER.**

**(b) The CEO office is optional — and this is the escape route.** Line 365 is **disjunctive**
("Directors **or** ... Chief Executive Officer") and line 367 says the Shareholders or Directors
"**may** appoint". Nothing in the Articles requires the company to have a CEO.

**(c) "The broadest powers to act in all circumstances" is wrong for this company** — see §6.

**Recommended treatment — the simplest legally sound answer:**

> **Do not have a CEO.** Delete the CEO definition [33], the second limb of [365], and the whole of
> [367]. Replace [365] with:
>
> *"Subject to the Companies Regulations and these Articles, the business of the Company shall be
> managed by the Directors, who may exercise all the powers of the Company."*

This resolves the residency blocker, resolves the CEO IIN blocker, resolves the chain-of-command
ambiguity (§6), removes an entire office from a one-person company, and shortens the document.
It is squarely within the mandate's guiding objective: *do not preserve clauses merely because they
are already drafted.*

**Caveats to confirm before deleting:**
- `UNCONFIRMED`: whether AFSA's registration form or portal **requires** a CEO to be nominated for
  a Private Company. Commentary suggests the CEO is a feature of the model articles rather than a
  statutory requirement, but this must be confirmed. Confirming step: the Registrar's incorporation
  form and AFSA registration guidance.
- If a CEO **is** required, the office must go to a Kazakhstan-resident IIN holder, and clause 367
  must then be **narrowed** (see §6), because it would otherwise place a resident third party above
  the owner-director.

### 5.4 NATURE OF BUSINESS [line 114] — the objects clause
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION**

> "The principal activities of the Company are the provision of advisory services **in the fields
> of finance**, corporate governance and business development, as well as the provision of other
> advisory and support services to legal entities and individuals both within and outside the
> AIFC."

**Two distinct problems.**

**(a) Regulatory risk.** The company is **not** seeking an AFSA licence and intends to carry on no
Regulated Activity [established fact]. Yet its constitution states that its **principal activity**
is advisory services "in the fields of finance". Advising on financial products, arranging deals in
investments and similar activities are **Regulated Activities** requiring an AFSA licence. A
constitution that describes the company's principal business in those terms invites AFSA to ask
whether a licence is required, and invites a bank to classify the company as a financial-services
business — with materially heavier onboarding and ongoing monitoring.

`UNCONFIRMED`: the precise scope of "Regulated Activities" under the AIFC General Rules /
Framework Regulations. Confirming step: AIFC GEN rules Schedule of Regulated Activities — not
reachable from this environment. The **risk** is nonetheless clear and cheap to avoid.

**(b) The objects do not describe the actual business.** The intended business is **sustainability
advisory to UN agencies and multilateral development banks** [established fact]. The word
"sustainability" does not appear anywhere in the 607 lines. The mismatch will surface at least
three times: in AFSA's activity classification, in bank KYC (where the business description on the
account application will not match the constitution), and in any future tender where a client
diligences the entity.

**Recommended treatment:**

> "The principal activities of the Company are the provision of advisory, consultancy, research,
> training and support services, including in relation to sustainability, environmental, social
> and governance matters, climate and development policy, corporate governance, business
> development and project management, to international organisations, multilateral development
> banks, governmental and intergovernmental bodies, and to legal entities and individuals, both
> within and outside the Astana International Financial Centre.
>
> The Company shall not carry on any Regulated Activity or Market Activity within the meaning of
> the AIFC Regulations and Rules unless and until duly licensed to do so by the Astana Financial
> Services Authority."

The second paragraph is the important addition: an express negative statement is worth more than a
carefully worded positive one.

### 5.5 SHARE CAPITAL [line 128]
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: FACT + LEGAL INTERPRETATION**

The clause is a single 70-word sentence carrying four separate defects.

**(a) Arithmetic.** 100 × KZT 545.5 = **KZT 54,550**, not the stated KZT 54,450. Implied rates
544.50 vs 545.50 — consistent with a digit transposition.

**(b) Amount in words.** "five hundred forty five tenge and **five tiyn**" = KZT 545.05, implying a
**third** total of KZT 54,505. Since words conventionally prevail over figures in monetary
instruments, this is the most likely operative — and it matches nothing else in the package.
Replicated in Russian [130]. Full analysis at **D03 §6**.

**(c) Authorised versus issued — a conceptual confusion.** The clause opens *"The **authorised**
share capital of the Company is EUR 100"* and closes *"the total nominal value of the **issued and
allotted** shares amounts to KZT 54 450"*. These are different concepts, equated in one sentence.
"Authorised capital" is a legacy ceiling concept; AIFC practice works with **issued** capital, and
**[T1-snippet]** the requirement is that each share have a fixed nominal value and not be allotted
below it. If "authorised" is read as a ceiling, the company **cannot issue a single further share**
without a Special Resolution amending the Articles — an unnecessary restriction on a company that
may well want to capitalise later.

**(d) Fixing the total in the constitution is itself an unnecessary restriction.** Every future
issue would require an amendment under [586], with the Special Resolution and director's compliance
certificate that entails.

**Recommended treatment — redraft, do not repair:**

> "The share capital of the Company is divided into ordinary shares of a nominal value of
> **KZT 1,000** each, each share conferring one vote and ranking equally in all respects.
>
> On incorporation, **100** ordinary shares are issued and allotted to Wiese Advisory GmbH,
> CHE-212.321.848, as sole Shareholder, representing an issued share capital of **KZT 100,000**."

This: removes the arithmetic (100 × 1,000 = 100,000, verifiable at a glance); removes the tiyn;
removes the FX reference and its permanent staleness; removes "authorised"; states the class rights;
and — importantly — **names the allottee**, curing the gap identified at D03 §4.5. AIFC imposes no
minimum capital **[T1-snippet]**, so the figure is presentational.

### 5.6 LANGUAGE [lines 590–591] — self-contradictory
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION**

> [590] "These Articles of Association are executed in English and Russian, **both versions having
> equal legal force**."
> [591] "In the event of any discrepancy, inconsistency or difference in interpretation between the
> English and Russian versions, **the English version shall prevail**."

The two sentences cannot both be true. "Equal legal force" means neither yields to the other;
"English shall prevail" means the Russian yields. Read together, a Russian-speaking court or
counterparty has a live argument that sentence 590 is the parties' primary statement and 591 an
inconsistent afterthought.

This matters more here than in most contracts, because this review has identified **at least six
substantive EN/RU divergences** in this document alone (§4 items 7–11, plus the numbering
divergence at §2 and the "директор"/CEO conflation at [373]). The precedence rule is not
theoretical — it will be needed.

**Recommended treatment: delete [590]; keep [591], expanded:**

> "These Articles of Association are executed in English and in Russian. In the event of any
> discrepancy, inconsistency or difference in interpretation between the two versions, **the
> English version shall prevail**."

If the counterparty insists on equal force for filing reasons, then the two texts must be
**reconciled line by line** before execution, and every divergence in §4 must be closed — a
materially larger job. Prevailing-language is the cheaper and safer route.

## 6. Chain of command: does the CEO clause conflict with the Directors' and Shareholders' powers?

**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION**
**Evidence: lines 365, 367, 379; and Resolution lines 21, 23, 26**

**Yes. As drafted the Articles create a three-way ambiguity, and the ambiguity is worse — not
better — because one person fills every role.**

### 6.1 The three competing power sources

| Source | Text | Power claimed |
|---|---|---|
| **Directors** [365] | "the business of the Company **must be managed by the Directors** or by another natural person ... bearing the title of Chief Executive Officer" | General management |
| **CEO** [367] | "shall have **the broadest powers to act in all circumstances** in the name of the Company, within the limits of the corporate objects and subject to powers expressly reserved **by law** for Shareholders' meetings and the Directors" | General management **plus** representation of the company to third parties |
| **Shareholders** [379] | "may, **by Special Resolution**, direct the Directors to take, or refrain from taking, specified action" | Direction — **but only of the Directors** |

### 6.2 The three conflicts

**(a) Directors vs CEO.** Line 365 is disjunctive: management vests in the Directors **or** in the
CEO. It does not say which prevails when both exist. Line 367 then gives the CEO "the broadest
powers to act in all circumstances", subject only to powers reserved **"by law"** — note, *by law*,
not *by these Articles*. So the Directors' management power under [365], which is a power conferred
**by the Articles**, is arguably **not** among the reservations that limit the CEO. Read strictly,
the CEO outranks the board on everything not reserved by statute.

**(b) Shareholders vs CEO.** The reserve power at [379] permits the Shareholders to direct **"the
Directors"**. It says nothing about directing the **CEO**. If management has vested in the CEO
under [365], the shareholders' reserve power may direct an organ that is no longer managing the
company. **The owner's only constitutional lever does not reach the person holding the broadest
powers.**

**(c) Third-party representation.** Line 367's final sentence — "He shall represent the Company in
its dealings with third parties" — is unqualified. A third party dealing with the CEO could rely on
it notwithstanding any internal limitation the Directors purport to impose.

### 6.3 Why one person filling all roles makes this worse, not better

It is tempting to say the ambiguity is harmless because Alexander Wiese would be Director, CEO and
Authorised Signatory simultaneously [Res. lines 21, 23, 26]. The opposite is true, for four reasons:

1. **The Articles will outlive the coincidence.** The moment a Kazakhstan-resident CEO is appointed
   — which is exactly what line 367 requires, and which the company may be forced into if the
   residency clause is retained — a third party holds "the broadest powers to act in all
   circumstances in the name of the Company" and can represent it to third parties, while the
   owner's reserve power [379] reaches only the Directors. **The clause that blocks Alexander Wiese
   from being CEO is the same clause that makes a resident CEO dangerous.**
2. **Internal limits do not bind third parties.** Whatever the Director instructs the CEO, [367]
   holds the CEO out to the world as the company's representative.
3. **Formal decision-making becomes untraceable.** Where one person holds three offices under
   overlapping grants, no minute records *which* office acted. On a later dispute, audit or
   sale, it may be impossible to show that a decision was validly taken.
4. **The "Authorised Signatory" appointment [Res. line 26] adds a fourth, wholly undefined role**
   that appears nowhere in these Articles. See D03 §4.3.

### 6.4 Recommended treatment

**Primary recommendation — abolish the CEO office** (§5.3). This dissolves all three conflicts at
once: management vests unambiguously in the Directors [365 as amended]; the Shareholders' reserve
power [379] reaches the only managing organ; and third-party representation follows the Directors.
Chain of command: **Shareholder → Director → (delegation under Art. 20 if ever needed)**. One line,
no ambiguity.

**Secondary recommendation — if a CEO must be retained**, make three amendments:

1. Change [365] to make the CEO's authority **derivative**: *"The business of the Company shall be
   managed by the Directors. The Directors may appoint a Chief Executive Officer to whom they may
   delegate such of their powers as they think fit, on such terms as they think fit, and may revoke
   or vary any such delegation at any time."*
2. Delete "the broadest powers to act in all circumstances" from [367] and substitute *"such powers
   as are delegated to him by the Directors from time to time"*.
3. Extend [379] so the Shareholders may direct **"the Directors and any Chief Executive Officer"**,
   and reduce the threshold from Special to **Ordinary Resolution**.
