## 2. The defect at line 12: "I are the duly appointed Director"

**Evidence:** line 12 (EN); line 14 (RU)

> "I, Alexander Wiese do hereby certify that **I are** the duly appointed Director of Wiese
> Advisory GmbH ... and that the resolution set forth below was duly adopted **by myself** on 25th
> July 2026 ... and is in conformity with the provisions of the **bye-laws** of the Company"

This single sentence contains four distinct problems, of escalating seriousness.

### 2.1 "I are" — grammatical
**Severity: TYPOGRAPHICAL · FINDING TYPE: FACT**

Subject/verb disagreement. On its own, trivial. Its significance is **evidential**: "I are" is the
residue of a template that read "**we are** the duly appointed Directors" — a plural,
multi-director form, partially converted to the singular. Taken with the metadata (created
2020-04-30, revision 39, original author Anara Omarova), it shows the document was adapted rather
than drafted, and that the adaptation was not proofread.

For a constitutional instrument that will be filed on a public register, relied on by a bank, and
possibly produced in evidence years later, an uncorrected grammatical error on the first
substantive line is a reputational and, marginally, an authenticity problem. **Correct it.**

### 2.2 A Director purports to adopt a **shareholder's** resolution
**Severity: CRITICAL · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION**

The document is titled a resolution of the **shareholder** (EN) / **founder** (RU). Its operative
certification says the resolution was adopted by Alexander Wiese **in his capacity as Director**
of the GmbH.

A director is not the shareholder. Under Swiss law the GmbH's decision-making organ for
shareholder-level matters is the **Gesellschafterversammlung** (quotaholders' meeting), or the
sole quotaholder acting alone; the **Geschäftsführer** is the managing organ. The decision to
incorporate a subsidiary, subscribe for its shares and appoint its officers is capable of being
taken by either organ depending on the GmbH's Statuten and the amounts involved — but the
instrument must **say which organ acted**, because that is what establishes its validity.

Here, the person is the same in both capacities (Alexander Wiese is sole quotaholder and sole
managing officer), so the defect is **curable on the same facts** — but it is not cured by
silence. As drafted, the instrument records the wrong organ.

**Recommendation:** replace the certificate wrapper with a direct written resolution:

> "**WRITTEN RESOLUTION OF WIESE ADVISORY GmbH**, CHE-212.321.848, a company incorporated under
> the laws of the Swiss Confederation, in its capacity as **sole incorporating shareholder of
> Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd.** (a Private Company to be incorporated in the Astana International
> Financial Centre).
>
> Wiese Advisory GmbH, acting by its sole quotaholder and by its Managing Director
> (*Geschäftsführer*) Alexander Wiese, who is duly authorised to represent the company [alone /
> jointly with X] pursuant to the entry in the Commercial Register of the Canton of Zug, HEREBY
> RESOLVES:"

### 2.3 "bye-laws"
**Severity: MEDIUM · FINDING TYPE: FACT · Evidence: line 12 (EN); line 14 (RU)**

"**bye-laws**" is Bermuda / Cayman Islands / BVI terminology for a company's internal
constitution. It has no application to a Swiss GmbH, whose constitution is its **Statuten**
(articles of association), nor to an AIFC company, whose constitution is its **Articles of
Association**.

Note that the **Russian mirror is correct**: line 14 says «положениям **Устава** Компании». So the
English carries the offshore template's language while the Russian carries the right concept — a
divergence in the certification clause itself.

Combined with the 2020 creation date and 39 revisions, this is direct textual evidence that the
form originated in an offshore practice and has been reused across jurisdictions without a proper
conforming pass. That matters beyond tidiness: it raises the question of **what else** in the form
was not adapted. On this review, the answer includes the plural/singular residue (§2.1), the
"Company" definition failure (§1.4) and the actor error at line 29 (§3).

### 2.4 The certificate wrapper is inappropriate here
**Severity: MEDIUM · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: line 12**

The "I hereby certify that the resolution set forth below was duly adopted ... has not been
modified or rescinded ... is now in full force and effect" formula is a **secretary's / officer's
certificate**. It exists so that an officer can vouch to a third party for a decision taken by
*another* organ (typically a board or a general meeting) whose minutes the third party will never
see.

Here, the certifier, the decision-maker and the signatory are **the same natural person**. The
wrapper adds no assurance; it adds only the three defects at §2.1–2.3, plus a fourth: it makes the
document *evidence about* a resolution rather than *being* the resolution, which invites the
question "may we see the underlying resolution of 25 July?" — a question the package cannot
answer.

**Recommendation:** drop the certificate wrapper entirely. A sole shareholder resolves in writing;
that writing **is** the instrument. If AFSA or a bank later wants certification, it can be given
separately and dated on the day it is given.

## 3. Line 29: "RESOLVED, that **the Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd.** duly adopts proposed Articles of Association"

**Severity: CRITICAL · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION**
**Evidence:** line 29 (EN); line 30 (RU); AoA lines 1–4

Three separate errors in one clause.

### 3.1 Wrong actor — the company cannot adopt its own founding constitution

A company's first Articles of Association are adopted by its **incorporators / subscribers**, and
they take effect on incorporation. The company has no legal personality before registration and
therefore no capacity to resolve anything. Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd. cannot adopt the document
that brings it into existence.

The correct actor is **Wiese Advisory GmbH**, as sole incorporating shareholder — which is exactly
what the AoA cover page itself says: *"Approved by Resolution Of the Shareholder of Wiese Advisory
GmbH"* [AoA lines 1–3]. The two documents therefore **disagree with each other** about who adopts
the Articles: the AoA says the shareholder does; the Resolution says the new company does.

(The AoA cover page carries its own version of the §1.1 defect — "Resolution **Of the Shareholder
Of** Wiese Advisory GmbH" again reads as the shareholder *of* the GmbH. See D04.)

### 3.2 Wrong company incorporated

Per §1.4, "the Company" is defined as the GmbH, so the clause resolves upon "incorporation of
[Wiese Advisory GmbH] in the Astana International Financial Centre".

### 3.3 "the" before a proper name

"**the** Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd." — the definite article precedes the company's name at lines
15, 18, 23, 26 and 29. Consistent, and consistently wrong. TYPOGRAPHICAL, but it appears five
times in a two-page document that will be filed publicly.

### 3.4 Recommended replacement

> "RESOLVED, that Wiese Advisory GmbH, as sole incorporating shareholder, hereby **adopts and
> approves** the Articles of Association of Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd. in the form annexed to
> this resolution, to take effect upon the incorporation of that company in the Astana
> International Financial Centre; and that the Articles of Association be executed on behalf of
> Wiese Advisory GmbH and filed with the Registrar of Companies of the AIFC."

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL REQUIREMENT (to be confirmed).** Where a company does **not** adopt the
AIFC Standard Articles in their entirety, the incorporators must submit to the Registrar, **before
the Articles are adopted**, a statement that the proposed Articles comply with the Companies
Regulations, the Rules and all other applicable AIFC Regulations and Rules. **[T1-snippet — AIFC
Companies Regulations, articles-of-association provisions]** The draft Articles depart from the
Standard Articles (name, registered office, objects, share capital, language clause, signature
block), so this statement appears to be required — and **it is not in the package**. Confirming
step: the Registrar's prescribed incorporation form and the Companies Rules. See D04.

### 4.4 The IIN question — the one legal point that needs primary confirmation
**Severity: BLOCKER (pending confirmation) · FINDING TYPE: UNCONFIRMED**

Both the Director requirement [AoA line 358] and the CEO definition [AoA line 33] require an
**Individual Identification Number**. Alexander Wiese has none.

Critically, **both provisions appear to be copied verbatim from the AIFC Standard Articles of
Association for Private Companies (Schedule 5)**. The Standard Articles are reported to provide:
*"The Company must have at least 1 Director, who is a natural person and has an Individual
Identification Number"*, and to define the CEO as *"the chief executive officer of the Company,
who is a natural person and has an Individual Identification Number"*. **[T1-snippet — AIFC
Companies Rules, Schedule 5.]** Secondary commentary indicates there is **no residency requirement
for directors**, but that a foreign national appointed as director must **obtain an IIN**, applied
for through the Expat Centre. **[Tier 3 commentary — must not override primary law.]**

The distinction that must be resolved before filing is:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the IIN requirement a **Regulations/Rules-level requirement** binding on every AIFC company, or merely **model-form wording** in Schedule 5 that a bespoke set of Articles may omit? | If model-form only, the Articles can be redrafted to remove it and the Director appointment stands. If Regulations-level, **Alexander Wiese must obtain an IIN before he can be appointed Director** — and that becomes the critical-path item for the whole incorporation. |
| Same question for the CEO **residency** requirement at AoA line 367 | If model-form only, deleting the CEO office (§4.2 option 1) resolves it. |

Because the draft Articles already deviate from the Standard Articles in several places, the
company is **not** adopting Schedule 5 in its entirety — so it is, in principle, free to depart
from it, subject to the incorporators' compliance statement (§3.4).

**Action required:** put both questions to AFSA / Kazakh counsel in writing, and in parallel begin
the IIN application for Alexander Wiese, since it is likely to be required for the Director role
in any event and has a lead time.

## 5. The three dates

**Evidence:** adoption **25 July 2026** [line 12]; document dated **11 August 2026** [lines 5–6];
FX rate "effective as of **31 July 2026**" [line 32]. Cross-reference: AoA cover **11 August 2026**
[AoA line 4]; PoA **11 August 2026** [PoA lines 3–6].

### 5.1 Deliberate, acceptable, or a filing problem?

**Answer: a filing problem — and specifically, an internal impossibility.**
**Severity: CRITICAL · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION**

A certificate-of-resolution structure legitimately carries two dates: the date the resolution was
*adopted*, and the later date on which an officer *certifies* it. So 25 July (adoption) and
11 August (certification) are, in themselves, a normal and acceptable pattern.

The problem is the **third** date. Line 32 — which is inside the resolved text, i.e. inside what
was purportedly adopted on **25 July 2026** — fixes the share capital by reference to the National
Bank of Kazakhstan's official exchange rate **"effective as of 31 July 2026"**.

**A resolution adopted on 25 July cannot resolve by reference to an exchange rate that did not
exist until 31 July.** Only two explanations are possible, and both are damaging:

| Explanation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| (a) The resolution was **not** adopted on 25 July; the adoption date is wrong | The certification at line 12 is inaccurate as to the date |
| (b) The resolution was adopted on 25 July and **subsequently amended** to insert the FX reference | The certification at line 12 that the resolution *"has not been modified or rescinded"* is **false** |

Either way, the document as filed cannot be reconciled on its own face. A reviewer at AFSA, and
certainly a bank compliance officer, can spot this by reading two lines of the same page.

### 5.2 Cross-document date conflict
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: FACT**

The Articles state on their cover: *"Approved by Resolution Of the Shareholder of Wiese Advisory
GmbH. **Dated 11 August 2026**"* [AoA lines 1–4]. The Resolution states the resolution was
**adopted 25 July 2026** and merely certified on 11 August.

So the Articles identify their approving resolution as being **dated 11 August**; the Resolution
says it is dated **25 July**. The two filed documents do not agree on the date of the instrument
that connects them. **Recommendation:** collapse to a single date. Re-execute the resolution on
one date, use the NBK rate for that date (or eliminate FX entirely — §6.4), and make the AoA cover
page match.

## 6. Share capital: independent recomputation

**Evidence:** Resolution line 32 (EN), line 33 (RU); AoA line 128 (EN), line 130 (RU) — the two
documents carry substantively identical wording and therefore identical errors.

### 6.1 The four figures the document states

| # | Statement | Line |
|---|---|---|
| A | Contribution of **EUR 100** | 32 |
| B | "which will be equal to **KZT 54 450**" at the NBK rate of 31 July 2026 | 32 |
| C | **100** ordinary shares | 32 |
| D | Nominal value **KZT 545,5** each, in words *"five hundred forty five tenge and **five tiyn**"* | 32 |
| E | Total nominal value of issued and allotted shares: **KZT 54 450** | 32 |

### 6.2 Independent recomputation

| Basis | Calculation | Total | Implied EUR/KZT rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stated total** (B and E) | given | **KZT 54,450** | **544.50** |
| **Digits** (C × D-as-digits) | 100 × 545.5 | **KZT 54,550** | **545.50** |
| **Words** (C × D-as-words) | 100 × 545.05 | **KZT 54,505** | **545.05** |

**FINDING TYPE: FACT. The document implies three mutually inconsistent totals and three
different exchange rates.** The lead reviewer's brief identifies the first two; the third — arising
from the incorrect amount-in-words — is a further and independent inconsistency.

- **Discrepancy between stated total and digits: KZT 100** (54,550 − 54,450).
- **Discrepancy between stated total and words: KZT 55** (54,505 − 54,450).

### 6.3 The amount-in-words error

**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: FACT · Evidence: Resolution line 32 (EN), line 33 (RU); AoA line 128 (EN), line 130 (RU)**

1 tenge = 100 tiyn. Therefore **KZT 545.5 = five hundred forty-five tenge and FIFTY tiyn.**

The documents say **"five tiyn"** / «пять тиын» — which denotes KZT 545.**05**.

This is not a harmless slip. In Kazakhstan, as in most civil-law practice, where figures and words
conflict in a monetary instrument, **the words prevail**. On that convention the nominal value is
KZT 545.05, the total is KZT 54,505, and both stated totals (KZT 54,450) are wrong.

The error is **replicated in the Russian** at Resolution line 33 and AoA line 130
(«пятьсот сорок пять теңге и пять тиын»), so there is no correct version anywhere in the package.

Correct rendering: *"five hundred forty-five tenge and fifty tiyn"* / «пятьсот сорок пять тенге
пятьдесят тиын».

### 6.4 Root cause and recommended fix

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION.** The two implied rates — **544.50** and **545.50** — differ
by exactly 1.00, consistent with a **digit transposition** in the decimal position (44 ↔ 45). One
of the two is the true NBK rate for 31 July 2026; the other is a typing error propagated through
both documents.

`UNCONFIRMED`: which of 544.50 or 545.50 was the actual NBK EUR/KZT rate on 31 July 2026. This
review does not assert either. Confirming step: the National Bank of Kazakhstan official rates
archive (`nationalbank.kz`) — not reachable from this environment.

**Recommendation — do not repair the arithmetic; remove the problem.**
**FINDING TYPE: RECOMMENDATION.**

The cleanest fix is to stop denominating the capital in two currencies at a historical FX rate:

Additional reasons to abandon the FX construction:

- A **fractional nominal value** (KZT 545.5) requires tiyn precision. Tiyn are not in practical
  circulation. Every future calculation — dividends, transfers, any capital alteration under AoA
  line 214 — inherits the fraction.
- The FX reference is **permanently stale**. It fixes a constitutional figure to a rate on a single
  past day, and will look increasingly odd on the public register.
- **[T1-snippet]** Each share must have a **fixed nominal value** and may not be allotted below it.
  A round nominal value makes compliance self-evident.
