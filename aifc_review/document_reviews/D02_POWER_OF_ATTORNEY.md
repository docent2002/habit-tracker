## 1. Executive answer to the mandate's question

> *Should a registration PoA allow external attorneys to change the company's constitutional
> documents or increase capital without a fresh shareholder resolution?*

**No — but not for the reason usually given, and not as a flat prohibition.** The correct answer
has three parts, and each is reasoned below in §4.

1. **Increasing share capital: no, unconditionally.** There is no registration-stage need for it
   whatsoever, and the power is dangerous. **Delete.**
2. **Approving amendments to the Articles: no.** Approval is a shareholder decision, not an act of
   execution. A power of attorney is an instrument for *executing and delivering* decisions
   already taken; it is not an instrument for *taking* them. Conflating the two removes the only
   control the Principal retains. **Delete the word "approve".**
3. **Executing and filing amendments: yes, but narrowly.** A blanket refusal here would be
   impractical drafting advice. AFSA review routinely produces Registrar comments on the Articles
   during the application. If the attorneys cannot re-execute a corrected text, every comment
   requires a fresh notarised and apostilled instrument from Zug — weeks of delay and repeated
   cost, for changes the Principal has already approved by email. **Retain, but confine to
   amendments *required by or made at the request of* the Registrar or AFSA in the course of the
   incorporation application, and *approved in writing by the Principal* before filing.**

The distinction that does the work is **decision versus execution**. Line 81 currently grants
both. It should grant only the second, and only for the registration window.

## 3. Is the PoA broader than necessary for a registration mandate?

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION. Yes — but in only four specific respects.** The document
is, on the whole, a competent and unusually well-controlled instrument. It is important to say
this plainly, because an indiscriminate "the PoA is too broad" would be wrong and would waste the
drafter's good work.

**Proportionate and properly scoped (roughly 85% of the document):**
- The incorporation powers [lines 24–34] are exactly what an incorporation agent needs, no more.
- The AFSA-representation powers [lines 30–31] are the core of the mandate.
- The KYC powers [lines 39–43] are correctly framed as *informational and procedural* — submit,
  correspond, attend, be identified — with no transactional authority.
- Line 44 is a model of self-limiting drafting: signature "**solely** for the purpose of opening
  bank accounts", and only "where such execution is permitted by the internal requirements of the
  relevant bank". It defers to the bank's own controls rather than overriding them.
- The negative covenants [lines 66–73] are excellent (see §5).

**Over-broad in four respects:**

| # | Over-breadth | Line | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Power to **increase share capital** — of an undefined "Company" | 81 | **CRITICAL** |
| 2 | Power to **approve** (not merely execute) amendments to the Articles | 81 | HIGH |
| 3 | **Right of substitution**, unrestricted and unnotified | 83 | HIGH |
| 4 | Purported authority over **the company being incorporated** and over "subsequent corporate maintenance" — which the Principal has no power to grant | 16, 36 | HIGH |

The catch-all at line 81 is **not** among them; see D02-08.

## 4. Detailed findings

### D02-01 — "increase the share capital of **the Company**" — "the Company" is never defined
**Severity: CRITICAL · FINDING TYPE: FACT + LEGAL INTERPRETATION**
**Evidence:** PoA lines 81 (EN), 82 (RU); definitions at lines 7 ("Principal") and 14 ("Attorneys")

The PoA defines exactly two terms: **"Principal"** (Wiese Advisory GmbH) and **"Attorneys"**.
It **never defines "the Company"**. Yet "the company" / "the Company" is used at lines 26, 28, 36,
39, 71, 73 and — critically — at line 81.

At lines 26–39 the context makes the intended referent clear enough (the AIFC company being
incorporated). At line 81 it does not. Read literally, the only entity that is *a company*, that
*exists*, and that *has share capital capable of being increased* on the date of execution is
**Wiese Advisory GmbH itself** — the Principal. On that reading the Principal has authorised two
Kazakh attorneys to increase the capital of the Swiss parent.

The Russian text is worse, not better: line 82 reads simply "увеличивать уставный капитал" —
**with no possessive at all**. Whose capital is not stated in the operative Russian text, which is
the text a Kazakh notary or bank will read.

This is not a theoretical reading. It is what the words say. Combined with the substitution right
(D02-06), an unnamed substitute could present this instrument as authority over the parent
company's capital.

**Recommendation:** delete the capital power entirely (see D02-02), and separately **define "the
Company"** at first use as "the Private Company to be incorporated in the AIFC under the name
Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd. (or such other name as may be reserved)".

### D02-02 — Power to increase share capital has no registration-stage justification
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: PoA line 81**

Even read benevolently — as referring to the AIFC company — the power is unjustifiable in a
registration mandate:

1. **It is not needed.** The capital is fixed by the incorporating shareholder before
   registration [Resolution line 32] and stated in the Articles [AoA line 128]. Nothing in an
   incorporation application calls for an increase.
2. **It is a shareholder decision.** Under the draft Articles, alteration of share capital
   requires an **Ordinary Resolution** of the shareholders [AoA line 214]. The PoA lets the
   attorneys bypass that.
3. **It is economically dangerous.** An increase means new shares. Nothing in the PoA says the
   new shares must be allotted to the Principal or that the Principal must remain the sole
   shareholder. As drafted, the attorneys could increase the capital and allot to a third party,
   **diluting the GmbH from 100% to a minority** — destroying the very structure the Corporate
   Structure chart (D01) exists to certify.
4. **It creates a funding obligation.** Increased capital must be paid up. The PoA nowhere says
   the attorneys may not commit the Principal to a payment obligation.
5. **The compounding effect.** The capital power is granted alongside a right of substitution
   (D02-06) and with **no corporate negative covenants** (D02-10) — unlike the banking powers,
   which are ringed with six express prohibitions.

**Recommendation: delete outright.** If a capital increase is ever genuinely needed
pre-registration (it will not be), it should be effected by a fresh shareholder resolution and,
if execution in Kazakhstan is required, a fresh single-purpose PoA.

### D02-03 — Authority to sign the "Sole Shareholder's Resolution" conflates decision with execution
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: PoA line 29**

Line 29 authorises the attorneys to "prepare, sign and submit ... the Sole Shareholder's
Resolution". A sole shareholder's resolution **is the shareholder's decision itself** — it is the
constitutional act by which the shareholder appoints directors, adopts the Articles and fixes the
capital. Delegating its *signature* to an external agent delegates the decision.

In practice the package does not rely on this: the Resolution in the package is signed by
Alexander Wiese personally [Resolution lines 41, 46]. But the power remains on the face of the
instrument, and creates the risk of **two differently-worded resolutions circulating** — one
signed in Zug, one signed in Astana by an attorney under this PoA — with no mechanism for
reconciling them.

**Recommendation:** narrow to "**submit and file** the Sole Shareholder's Resolution executed by
the Principal" — remove "prepare" and "sign".

### D02-04 — Line 81 "approve, execute and file any amendments to the Articles of Association"
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: PoA line 81; AoA line 586**

This is the clause the mandate asks about, and it needs unpacking word by word.

| Word | Effect | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **approve** | The attorneys decide, in their own discretion, what the constitution says | **Unacceptable.** Amendment of the Articles requires a **Special Resolution** — 75% of shareholder votes [AoA lines 43, 586]. The PoA lets an agent substitute their judgment for the shareholder's on the company's constitution. Delete. |
| **execute** | The attorneys sign a text the Principal has settled | **Acceptable if bounded.** This is the legitimate need — see §1.3. |
| **file** | The attorneys lodge the executed text with the Registrar | **Acceptable.** Purely ministerial. |
| **any amendments** | Unlimited subject-matter | **Unacceptable.** "Any" covers the objects clause, the share capital, the transfer restrictions, the amendment clause itself — the entire constitution. |

Note the further problem: the Articles are amendable by **Special Resolution** [AoA line 586], and
the Registrar requires, on filing an amendment, a **certificate from at least one Director** that
the amendment complies with the Regulations and Rules **[T1-snippet — AIFC Companies Regulations,
amendment of articles]**. Neither the Special Resolution nor the director's certificate can be
supplied by these attorneys — they are neither shareholder nor director. So the clause purports
to grant a power that, in the ordinary case, **cannot lawfully be exercised** through this
instrument alone.

**Recommendation — replacement wording:**

> "to execute and file such amendments to the Articles of Association as are required by, or made
> at the request of, the Registrar of Companies of the AIFC or AFSA in the course of the
> incorporation application, **provided that the text of any such amendment has first been
> approved in writing (including by email) by the Principal**; and for the avoidance of doubt the
> Attorneys have no authority to approve, propose or effect any other amendment to the Articles
> of Association."

### D02-05 — Duplicated Articles-signing power across two clauses
**Severity: LOW · FINDING TYPE: FACT · Evidence: PoA lines 29, 81**

Line 29 says "prepare, **sign** and submit the Articles of Association"; line 81 says "to
**execute** the Articles of Association". Two clauses, different verbs, same power. In a document
that will be construed strictly by a notary and a registrar, duplication invites the argument
that the two were meant to differ. Consolidate into one clause.

### D02-06 — Right of substitution: unrestricted, unnotified, unlimited in scope
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: PoA line 83; RU line 86**

"with the right of substitution" / "с правом передоверия" is granted in five words, with:

- **no restriction on who** may be substituted — any person, including someone outside the law
  firm, unknown to and unvetted by the Principal;
- **no notification duty** to the Principal — the Principal may never learn who holds authority
  over its affairs;
- **no scope limitation** — the substitute receives *everything*, including the capital power
  (D02-01/02) and the AoA-amendment power (D02-04);
- **no term limitation** — presumably co-terminous with the 12 months, but not said;
- **no automatic termination on revocation** — not stated, though it should follow.

The two named attorneys were chosen: they are identified by full name and IIN [lines 10–11],
i.e. the Principal has satisfied itself as to *them*. Substitution discards that selection
entirely. It is the mechanism by which every other over-breadth in this document escapes the
Principal's control.

`UNCONFIRMED`: whether Kazakhstan Civil Code provisions on *передоверие* independently require
notice to the principal and identification of the substitute, and whether a substitution executed
in Kazakhstan under a Swiss-executed PoA requires separate notarisation. Confirming step: Civil
Code of the Republic of Kazakhstan, chapter on representation and power of attorney
(`adilet.zan.kz` — unreachable from this environment).

**Recommendation:** delete the substitution right. If the law firm insists (holiday cover,
illness), replace with:

> "The Attorneys may substitute their authority under this Power of Attorney only in favour of
> another employee or partner of [firm name], only in respect of the powers set out in
> paragraphs [incorporation and filing paragraphs], and only upon prior written notice to the
> Principal identifying the substitute by full name and IIN. No substitution may be made in
> respect of paragraphs [Articles / capital / bank-opening]. Any substitution terminates
> automatically on revocation or expiry of this Power of Attorney."

### D02-07 — The PoA purports to grant authority over a company the Principal does not control and which does not exist
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: PoA lines 16, 36, 85**

Line 36: "represent the Principal **and the company being incorporated** before banks and other
financial institutions."

A principal can only confer authority it possesses. Wiese Advisory GmbH cannot confer authority
to act for **Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd.**, because (a) that company does not yet exist, and
(b) once it does, it is a **separate legal person** whose agents can only be appointed by *it*.
The same defect infects "subsequent corporate maintenance" at line 16.

The drafter evidently sensed this, because **line 85 says so** — see D02-11. But line 85 is
phrased as an observation about what banks "may require", not as a limitation on the grant. The
result is an instrument that grants an ineffective power in one clause and hints at its
ineffectiveness in another.

Practical consequence: a Kazakh bank presented with this PoA for **the new company's** account
will very likely reject it, because the account holder (Eurasia Ltd.) is not the Principal. The
bank-opening powers at lines 36–44 — nine clauses, a quarter of the document — are therefore
largely **inoperative as drafted**.

### D02-08 — The catch-all at line 81 is acceptable and should not be deleted
**Severity: LOW · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION (positive) · Evidence: PoA lines 81, 82**

"perform any other legal and factual acts **reasonably necessary for the implementation of this
Power of Attorney**".

This is commonly flagged reflexively as an over-breadth. It is not. The words are **bounded by
their own purpose clause**: acts must be reasonably necessary *for the implementation of this
Power of Attorney*, so the catch-all cannot enlarge the enumerated purposes — it can only supply
incidental acts within them. The Russian mirror [line 82] carries the same bounding
("разумно необходимые для исполнения настоящей доверенности").

The real problem at line 81 is the *enumerated* capital and amendment powers, not the residual
sweep-up. Once those are removed, the catch-all is proportionate.

**Minor refinement only:** replace "any other legal and factual acts" with "any other acts
ancillary to the powers expressly granted above", to foreclose an argument that "legal acts"
imports a power to conclude contracts generally.

### D02-09 — Validity period: 12 months is too long for a registration mandate, and its start date is not evidenced
**Severity: MEDIUM · FINDING TYPE: PRACTICAL REQUIREMENT · Evidence: PoA lines 3–6, 83, 93, 97**

Line 83: "issued for a period of twelve (12) months **from the date of its execution**."

Two problems.

1. **Duration.** AIFC incorporation of a straightforward non-regulated Private Company is a
   matter of weeks. A 12-month grant leaves the instrument live long after the mandate is spent —
   and, because it also purports to cover "subsequent corporate maintenance" [line 16], it invites
   continued use. **Recommendation:** "until the earlier of (i) the date falling six months from
   execution and (ii) the date of issue of the Certificate of Incorporation of the Company,
   save that the powers in paragraph [receipt of documents] survive for 30 days thereafter."
2. **The start date is not evidenced on the face of the document.** The 12 months runs from
   *execution*, but the signature lines [93, 97] carry **no date field**. The only date is the
   heading date [lines 3–6, 11 August 2026], which is the date of *preparation*. Contrast the
   Corporate Structure chart, which does provide "Date of signature ____". A bank or registrar
   receiving this instrument in 2027 cannot determine from it whether it has expired.
   **Recommendation:** add a dated signature block.

### D02-10 — Revocation mechanics are incomplete
**Severity: MEDIUM · FINDING TYPE: PRACTICAL REQUIREMENT · Evidence: PoA line 83**

"may be revoked by the Principal at any time by written notice." Notice **to whom** is not stated.
The clause does not address:

- notice to the Attorneys **and** to each substitute;
- an obligation on the Attorneys to **return all originals and certified copies**;
- the Principal's ability to notify **AFSA, the Registrar and the banks** directly — third parties
  holding an apostilled original will otherwise continue to rely on it in good faith;
- **automatic termination of any substitution** on revocation;
- the number of **originals issued** — which matters greatly for an apostilled instrument, since
  each original circulates independently. The document does not say how many exist.

**Recommendation:** add a revocation clause covering all five points, and record the number of
originals on the face of the instrument.

### D02-11 — Line 85 is good drafting, but is a statement of fact where it should be a limitation
**Severity: LOW (positive finding with a refinement) · FINDING TYPE: FACT · Evidence: PoA line 85**

> "If, following the incorporation of the company in the AIFC, certain actions are required
> under applicable law or the internal requirements of a bank to be performed on behalf of the
> incorporated company itself, the relevant bank, governmental authority or service provider may
> require a separate power of attorney or corporate resolution issued by such company."

**This is a genuinely sensible clause and the drafter deserves credit for it.** It anticipates
precisely the separate-legal-person problem identified at D02-07, and it manages the client's
expectations honestly rather than over-promising what the instrument can do.

Its weakness is grammatical mood. It says a third party *may require* something. It does not say
that the Attorneys *have no authority* to act for the incorporated company. It therefore mitigates
the drafting error at line 36 without curing it.

**Recommendation:** keep it, and add:

> "For the avoidance of doubt, this Power of Attorney is granted by the Principal in its own name
> and in its capacity as incorporating shareholder only. It does not, and cannot, authorise the
> Attorneys to act for, represent or bind Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd. after its incorporation."

### D02-12 — Recommendation: split into two powers of attorney
**Severity: HIGH (structural) · FINDING TYPE: RECOMMENDATION**

**Yes — a separate post-incorporation PoA should be used, and the current combined instrument
should be split.** Four independent reasons:

1. **Different principals.** Pre-incorporation the only possible principal is the GmbH.
   Post-incorporation the relevant principal is **Eurasia Ltd.** — a different legal person. One
   instrument cannot do both jobs because the GmbH cannot speak for its subsidiary (D02-07).
2. **Different risk profiles.** Registration is time-boxed, low-value, and involves filing
   documents with a public registrar. Corporate maintenance and bank onboarding are open-ended and
   touch the company's money. They warrant different durations, different limitations and
   different negative covenants.
3. **Cleaner revocation.** Registration authority should die on the Certificate of Incorporation.
   Bundling it with maintenance keeps a spent power alive for a year.
4. **Bank practice.** A bank opening an account for Eurasia Ltd. will require a PoA **issued by
   Eurasia Ltd.**, executed by its director, under its own board/shareholder authority. A
   parent-issued PoA is routinely rejected. The current lines 36–44 are therefore likely to be
   wasted effort.

**Proposed structure:**

| | **PoA #1 — Registration** | **PoA #2 — Post-incorporation** |
|---|---|---|
| Principal | Wiese Advisory GmbH | Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd. |
| Executed | Zug, before incorporation; notarised + apostilled | Astana or Zug, after incorporation |
| Scope | Lines 24–34 (incorporation), 30–31 (AFSA representation), narrowed 81 (execute/file Registrar-required AoA amendments approved in writing) | Bank account opening, KYC, corporate maintenance, filings |
| Excluded | Capital increase; approval of AoA amendments; signing the shareholder resolution; substitution; all bank powers | Operating funds, payments, credentials, signatory changes, borrowing, account closure (i.e. lines 66–73 carried across) |
| Term | Earlier of 6 months or Certificate of Incorporation | 12 months, renewable |

This also resolves D02-07 at a stroke: nine clauses that are presently inoperative become
operative in the instrument where they belong.

### D02-13 — Notarisation and apostille: the document as drafted **cannot be apostilled**
**Severity: BLOCKER (for use in Kazakhstan) · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL REQUIREMENT + PRACTICAL REQUIREMENT**
**Evidence: PoA lines 88–97 — the entire execution block**

The execution block consists of, in full:

```
88  Principal:
89  Доверитель:
93  ____________________
97  ____________________
```

There is **no notarial certificate, no attestation clause, no witness block, no company stamp, no
name or title beneath either signature line, and no date field.**

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL REQUIREMENT.** Both Switzerland and Kazakhstan are parties to the **Hague
Convention of 5 October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public
Documents** (Kazakhstan acceded in 2001). An apostille therefore suffices between them and
consular legalisation is not required. **[T1-snippet — Hague Apostille Convention status table.]**

But an apostille authenticates the signature and seal of a **public official**. A power of
attorney signed privately by a company director is a **private document** and cannot be
apostilled as it stands. The sequence required is:

1. **Swiss notarisation in Zug** — the notary certifies (a) the authenticity of Alexander Wiese's
   signature and (b) **his authority to represent Wiese Advisory GmbH**, verified against a
   current commercial-register extract (see D02-14). A bare signature certification
   (*Beglaubigung der Unterschrift*) that does not confirm representative capacity is frequently
   rejected for corporate PoAs — ask the notary for a certification that covers capacity.
2. **Apostille** by the Staatskanzlei of the Canton of Zug, on the notarial certificate.
3. **Translation** — the document is already bilingual EN/RU, but Kazakh notaries and banks
   commonly require a translation certified by a Kazakhstan-registered translator whose signature
   is notarised locally, covering the *apostille and notarial certificate* as well as the body.
4. Retain the number of originals needed (AFSA, each bank, file) — see D02-10.

**Recommendation:** add a notarial block to the draft before it is sent to Zug, and confirm the
required form with the Zug notary in advance.

### D02-16 — EN/RU divergence: the Russian text arguably makes **Alexander Wiese personally** the Principal
**Severity: HIGH · FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION · Evidence: PoA lines 7 (EN), 8 (RU)**

English [line 7]:
> "issued by Wiese Advisory GmbH ... (hereinafter referred to as the "**Principal**"), represented
> by its Managing Director, Alexander Wiese, acting pursuant to the Articles of Association"

Russian [line 8]:
> "выдана Wiese Advisory GmbH ... (далее — «**Доверитель**»), в лице Директора Александра Визе,
> действующего на основании Устава, **именуемому в дальнейшем «Доверитель»**"

The Russian defines «Доверитель» **twice**. The second definition —
"именуемому в дальнейшем «Доверитель»" — is in the **dative singular masculine**, agreeing
grammatically with "Александра Визе / действующего", not with the company. On a plain reading of
the Russian, **Alexander Wiese personally** is designated the Principal.

This is not a stylistic quibble. If a Kazakh bank or notary reads the Russian as making the
individual the Principal, then the negative covenants at lines 66–73 (which restrain acts "on
behalf of the company") and the capital power at line 81 attach to the wrong person, and the
authority to incorporate "on behalf of the Principal" [line 24] would make **Alexander Wiese
personally**, not the GmbH, the incorporating shareholder — destroying the two-tier structure the
Corporate Structure chart certifies.

**Recommendation:** delete the second «именуемому в дальнейшем «Доверитель»» and confirm the
single definition attaches to the company.

### D02-19 — Missing boilerplate

## 5. The good drafting — recorded expressly

**FINDING TYPE: FACT (positive).** Three features of this document are better than one usually
sees, and should be preserved through any redraft.

### 5.1 The negative covenants [lines 66–73]

Six prohibitions, each well chosen and covering a real failure mode:

| Line | Prohibition | Why it is well chosen |
|---|---|---|
| 67 | No disposal or operation of account funds | The core protection: the attorneys open the account but never operate it |
| 68 | No payment instructions or payment documents | Closes the obvious workaround to line 67 |
| 69 | No bank cards, tokens, authentication devices, electronic keys, passwords or security credentials | **The best clause in the document.** Most PoAs restrict *authority* and forget *credentials*. Whoever holds the token holds the account regardless of what the PoA says. This clause is drafted by someone who has seen the failure mode |
| 70 | No amending or designating authorised signatories | Prevents the attorneys from installing themselves or a third party as signatory — i.e. prevents self-escalation |
| 71 | No loans, security, guarantees or other financial obligations | Prevents the company being encumbered |
| 73 | No closing accounts or otherwise disposing of funds | Closes the exit route |

Together these are a coherent, layered control: authority, instruments, credentials, signatory
identity, borrowing, and exit. Read as a set, they anticipate escalation rather than merely
listing prohibitions.

### 5.2 Line 44 — self-limiting signature power

"sign documents required **solely** for the purpose of opening bank accounts, **where such
execution is permitted by the internal requirements of the relevant bank**." This defers to the
bank's own controls instead of purporting to override them — a small clause that will save
argument at the counter.

### 5.3 Line 85 — the post-incorporation acknowledgment

See D02-11. Honest, correct, and rare.

### 5.4 The asymmetry that undermines the good work

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION.** The banking powers are ringed with six express
prohibitions. The **corporate** powers — which include increasing the capital of an undefined
company and amending the constitution — carry **none**. The drafter's evident care stopped at the
bank counter.

**Recommendation:** mirror lines 66–73 on the corporate side. The Attorneys shall not:

1. increase, reduce, consolidate, subdivide or otherwise alter the share capital of the Company
   or of the Principal;
2. allot, issue, transfer, pledge, encumber or dispose of any share in the Company;
3. approve, propose or effect any amendment to the Articles of Association, save as permitted by
   paragraph [narrowed line 81];
4. appoint, remove or replace any Director, Chief Executive Officer, Secretary or Authorised
   Signatory of the Company;
5. change the Company's name or registered office;
6. commit the Principal or the Company to any fee, expense or liability exceeding [amount] without
   the Principal's prior written approval;
7. accept service of, commence, compromise or settle any claim or proceeding;
8. substitute their authority, save as permitted by paragraph [narrowed line 83].
