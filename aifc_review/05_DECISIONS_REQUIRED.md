## Decision D01 — Do you proceed with the AIFC structure at all?

**Issue**
Your stated primary motive was tax. On the intended client base — sustainability advisory to UN
agencies and MDBs — the AIFC exemption is **not** obtained. Both limbs fail: the financial-services
limb does not list advisory, and the consulting limb reaches only services supplied *to AIFC Bodies
and AIFC Participants providing financial services*. Expect **20% Kazakhstan CIT on worldwide profit**
and **16% VAT** above ~KZT 41.48m. On the tax metric alone, doing the work from Zug is cheaper than
doing it from Astana.

**Option A — Proceed on the market-access rationale.** Accept the tax position. The AIFC still
delivers Central Asia access, an English common-law platform, AIFC courts, UN/MDB-facing credibility
and the Green Finance Centre ecosystem.

**Option B — Do not incorporate.** Serve Central Asian clients from Wiese Advisory GmbH directly.
Lowest cost; no KZ CIT layer, no payroll, no audit, no substance obligation.

**Option C — Defer.** Obtain E2–E4 (Kazakhstan tax counsel) first, then decide.

**Recommendation — C, then A.**
**Reason.** The tax conclusion is well corroborated but was reached under an egress constraint, and
one reviewer's *reasoning* was overturned on the primary text even though the conclusion held. A
written Kazakhstan tax view is cheap relative to the incorporation and to unwinding it. If the
market-access rationale is genuinely worth it to you — and for a UN/MDB-facing sustainability
practice it plausibly is — proceed with the tax question **answered rather than assumed**.
**Consequence of choosing differently.** Choosing A without E2–E4 risks discovering a 20% CIT charge
after you have committed. Choosing B forgoes a credible regional platform for a fixable tax outcome.

## Decision D02 — Do you appoint a CEO?

**Issue**
Your Articles (Schedule 5 Art. 18.2) require the CEO to be **resident in Kazakhstan**. The Resolution
appoints you — Swiss-resident. The package therefore appoints an officer its own constitution
disqualifies. Whether a CEO is mandatory at all is **genuinely unresolved**: Schedule 5 Art. 18.1 is
disjunctive ("Directors **or** … CEO") and "Chief Executive Officer" appears nowhere in the Companies
Regulations, but **AFSA's Feb-2025 Guidance §3.3 lists CEO under "Required appointments"** with no
optionality marker, while expressly marking the Secretary optional. Reviewer balance: **3:1 for
optional**, unresolved.

**Option A — Appoint no CEO.** You act as Director and Authorised Signatory. The residency clause
sits dormant. Cleanest if confirmed.

**Option B — Appoint a Kazakhstan-resident CEO.** Satisfies the clause and adds genuine local
substance (helpful for D01's tax and place-of-management questions) — but cedes executive authority
to a third party and imports a **mandatory Kazakhstan employment contract and salary**.

**Option C — Appoint yourself and delete the residency clause** (Route B drafting).

**Recommendation — A, conditional on E1.**
**Reason.** It is the cheapest and least intrusive, and the legal reading favours it. But **do not
build the file on it until the Registrar confirms in writing.** If the Registrar says mandatory, A is
impossible and the choice collapses to B or C.
**Consequence of choosing differently.** B introduces a salaried officer and a person with the
"broadest powers to act in all circumstances". C is workable but forces bespoke Articles (see D03).

## Decision D03 — Route A or Route B for the Articles?

**Issue**
Two recommended fixes in this review are **mutually exclusive**. Schedule 5 Art. 17 *is* the director
IIN requirement and Art. 18.2 *is* the CEO residency requirement — so "adopt Schedule 5 wholesale"
and "delete those clauses" cannot both be done. Your current draft occupies the **worst cell**: it is
bespoke (so it carries every COR 2.2.2 and s.14(5) obligation) while retaining precisely the two
clauses that disqualify you.

**Option A — Adopt Schedule 5 in its entirety.** Disapplies s.13(5)/s.14(5)/COR 2.2.2; minimal
Registrar scrutiny; resolves ~13 findings at a stroke. **But you adopt Art. 17, so you need an IIN to
hold office**, and you inherit six of AFSA's own drafting errors.

**Option B — Bespoke Articles with the IIN and residency limbs deleted.** No IIN needed to be a
Director; residency problem gone in both branches. **But** you must add COR 2.2.2(i) maximum
directors and (r) termination/liquidation, file the s.14(5) compliance statement, and accept
clause-by-clause scrutiny on a first filing.

**Recommendation — A if E1 confirms the CEO is optional and you are obtaining an IIN anyway;
otherwise B.**
**Reason.** You will need an IIN for the bank account, the e-signature and the entity BIN regardless,
so Art. 17 costs you little in practice — and Route A removes the most defects for the least
scrutiny. If the CEO turns out to be mandatory, Route A is impossible.
**Consequence of choosing differently.** Straddling the two — which is what the current draft does —
gives you Route B's exposure with Route A's content. That is the one outcome to avoid.

## Decision D04 — How wide should the attorneys' powers be?

**Issue**
The PoA lets the two Kazakhstan attorneys **increase share capital** and **approve, execute and file
amendments to the Articles**, with a **right of substitution** and a catch-all. That is far broader
than a registration mandate. But a flat ban would also be wrong: every Registrar comment would then
require a fresh notarised, apostilled instrument from Zug.

**Option A — Split decision from execution.** Remove the power to *approve* amendments and to
increase capital; retain the power to *execute and file* corrections the Registrar requires. Remove
or narrow substitution. Keep the banking negative covenants **verbatim** (they are well drafted) and
add a mirrored corporate set.

**Option B — Registration-only PoA, no amendment power at all.**

**Option C — Leave as drafted.**

**Recommendation — A, and split into two instruments:** a registration PoA from the GmbH, and a
separate post-incorporation PoA from the company once it exists.
**Reason.** The GmbH cannot lawfully confer authority over an entity that does not yet exist, which
makes nine bank-opening clauses inoperative as drafted and exposes the two attorneys to **personal
liability under s.31**. Splitting fixes both. Option B is safe but will cost you weeks on the first
Registrar query.
**Consequence of choosing differently.** C leaves external parties able to amend your constitution
and increase your capital, and leaves the attorneys personally on the hook.

## Decision D05 — Does a second Articles version exist?

**Issue**
The mandate referenced two Articles files; **only one was supplied**. No comparison was performed or
simulated. If two versions are circulating, the wrong one may be executed, apostilled or filed.

**Option A — Confirm only one version exists.** Record its MD5 (`5592357b3fa586049f281648a3501d2f`)
as the approved text.
**Option B — Supply the second version** and the comparison will be completed.

**Recommendation — A or B, but answer it before anything is signed.**
**Reason.** Version control over a constitutional document is not currently established.
**Consequence of choosing differently.** You may approve one text and file another.
