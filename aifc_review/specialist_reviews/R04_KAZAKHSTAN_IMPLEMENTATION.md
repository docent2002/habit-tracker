## 3. THE IIN — WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS AND WHAT IT ACTUALLY GATES

**`FACT`.** The Individual Identification Number is created under the **Law of the Republic of
Kazakhstan No. 223 of 12 January 2007 "On National Registers of Identification Numbers"**. It is the
universal natural-person key across all Kazakhstan state information systems. Since 1 January 2013 it
has been obligatory for residents and non-residents alike where they interact with those systems.

**`LEGAL REQUIREMENT`.** Without an IIN a foreign individual cannot, in the **national** RK
jurisdiction: be registered as a founder of a company, conclude an employment contract, or open and
operate bank accounts.

**The distinction the package has collapsed.** Those are **national-jurisdiction** consequences. An
AIFC private company is not registered in the national company register — AIFC entities are
registered with AFSA and receive a BIN in a dedicated AIFC range. So the sentence *"a foreigner
without an IIN cannot be registered as a founder"* — true of an RK **TOO/LLP** — **does not
automatically transfer to an AIFC private company**, and the shareholder here is in any event a Swiss
legal person, not Alexander personally.

**What the IIN really gates for this client:**

| Gate | IIN needed? | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Being **appointed** a Director of an AIFC private company | Not by the statutory director test; **required by Sch. 5 Art. 17 if that Article is adopted**; AFSA guidance is read by practitioners as requiring it for foreign directors — **conflict, see §9** | `UNCONFIRMED` |
| Being **appointed** CEO | Yes | `PRACTICAL`/rule |
| Obtaining an **EDS** | Yes, statutorily | `LEGAL` |
| Registering a phone in the **Mobile Citizens Database** | Yes | `PRACTICAL` |
| Opening the company's **bank account** (as first executive) | Yes | `LEGAL` + bank policy |
| Tying the entity **BIN to an entity EDS** | Yes — must be the first executive's own IIN | `LEGAL` |
| Filing the AFSA **incorporation application** | Not stated as a precondition anywhere located | `UNCONFIRMED` |

### 3.1 Can a non-resident foreigner obtain one, and how?

**Three routes were identified. They conflict on the central question of travel.**

| Route | Travel to KZ? | Time | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AIFC Expat Centre**, via the AIFC Portal (portal.aifc.kz) — the Centre has fully migrated to online service delivery | Application online. **Presence position disputed** | **3 business days** processing | Multiple AIFC sources; time figure consistently reported |
| **Public Service Centre (ЦОН)** in Kazakhstan, on a valid national passport | **Yes** | 1–3 working days | Consistently reported |
| **Kazakhstan embassy / consulate abroad** | No — Switzerland-side | Not stated | **Directly contradicted** — see below |

**`UNCONFIRMED` — MATERIAL CONFLICT.** One source states *"a foreigner may obtain IIN in consular
institutions"*; a specialist AIFC-registration practice states the opposite in terms: **"An IIN can
only be obtained within the territory of RK — this service is not available at consulates."** A third
source states flatly that *"the company's director must personally visit Kazakhstan to obtain an IIN,
as it cannot be obtained by power of attorney,"* while noting that a **BIN** *can* be obtained
remotely by PoA. A fourth notes the former egov.kz online IIN route has been **withdrawn**.

**This conflict is not resolvable on the evidence available to me and it is the single most
operationally important open question in this review.** It decides whether Alexander must fly to

### 3.2 The two other numbers that are called "IIN" but are not the same thing

**`FACT` — and a trap the package does not address.** There are now **three** distinct identifiers
in circulation, and adopting the wrong one is a live failure mode:

1. **State (non-resident) IIN** — the real Law-223 number, valid across all RK government systems.
   This is the one banks, the State Revenue Committee and the NCA (for EDS) need.
2. **AIFC e-IIN** — an AIFC-ecosystem identifier for foreign investors dealing with AIFC
   infrastructure, e.g. registering an AIFC company. **Reported as *not* a full equivalent of the
   state IIN and *not* usable for dealings with Kazakhstan government agencies.**
3. **e-Residency IIN** — Kazakhstan's e-Residency programme launched **1 January 2026**; fully remote,
   no travel, USD 499, delivering a Digital Identity Card, an IIN, a Kazakhstan eSIM, and reported
   ability to open a bank account and **register a company in the AIFC**. But: *"The e-Residency IIN
   is not analogous to the IIN issued to citizens… foreigners or stateless persons located in the
   Republic of Kazakhstan and **cannot be used in public services**."*

**`RECOMMENDATION` (HIGH).** e-Residency is the only genuinely travel-free route identified and it
directly advertises AIFC company registration and bank-account opening. **It should be investigated
first.** But its IIN is expressly not usable for public services — so it may not satisfy the NCA for
an EDS or the State Revenue Committee for tax filing. Confirm scope against the specific downstream
uses (§9 Item 3) before paying for it.

## 4. THE BIN

**`FACT` / `LEGAL REQUIREMENT`.** The Business Identification Number is the company's 12-digit RK
legal-entity identifier and tax number. For AIFC entities it is **assigned by the AIFC registration
authority**, issued **automatically on registration, free of charge**, and falls in a distinct AIFC
serial range (reported as 90001–99999 in positions 7–11) so that AIFC participants are identifiable
in any registry search.

**Practical consequence:** the BIN is **not a separate errand and not a blocker**. The PoA at line 32
("pay registration fees, state duties") already covers what is needed. The one trap is at row 20 of
the classification table: if an **entity-level EDS** is later wanted, the BIN application must carry
the **first executive's own IIN**, not an attorney's. Getting that wrong at BIN stage creates rework.

## 5. KAZAKHSTAN RESIDENCY — TESTED HARD

The Shared Brief asked me to test the AIFC's selling point that there is no KZ-residency requirement.
I did, from four different angles. **The selling point holds.**

1. **Statutory director test.** Reported as: natural person, ≥18, not disqualified, not an
   undischarged bankrupt. **No residency limb.**
2. **Express negative statements.** *"There are no requirements for director nationality or residency
   in AIFC private companies."* And, separately: *"AFSA has no requirement on presence of a C5 visa
   or temporary residence permit in case of registration of private companies."* The second is
   significant — it is the immigration-side confirmation that a non-resident may take office.
3. **Where residency *is* required, AIFC says so explicitly.** The **MLRO** function must be performed
   by an individual **ordinarily resident in the Republic of Kazakhstan**. That is a regulated-firm
   AML role, irrelevant to a non-regulated advisory company — but its existence proves the drafters
   of AIFC law know how to impose a residency test when they intend one, and did not impose one on
   directors.
4. **Ownership.** *"AIFC laws allow ownership structuring without any restrictions"* — 100% foreign
   ownership is unproblematic. The Swiss GmbH shareholder raises no issue.

**`LEGAL INTERPRETATION` — the correct characterisation of AoA Art. 18.2.** The residency sentence is
a **constitutional self-restriction**, not a legal requirement. On the evidence it originates in the
AFSA **Schedule 5 Standard Articles** — meaning the AIFC supplies it as a *default*, presumably on
the assumption that a company appointing a CEO intends a real, salaried, locally-employed executive
(which is coherent with row 8: the CEO must have an RK employment contract and salary).

Because the Companies Regulations permit a company to adopt the Standard Articles *"in whole or any
part,"* and require only a compliance statement from the Incorporators where they are not adopted
entirely, **the clause is deletable at the drafting stage at zero legal risk.** Deleting it is not a
derogation from law; it is the exercise of an option AIFC law expressly confers.

**Severity of the package as it stands:** the Resolution [Res. line 21] appoints as CEO a person the
proposed Articles [AoA line 367] disqualify. The company would be incorporated with a constitution its
own first executive breaches from day one. That is **CRITICAL** — not because it is unlawful, but
because it is a self-inflicted internal contradiction, discoverable on the face of two documents in
the same filing set, that the Registrar may well query and that would taint the company's first
corporate act.

## 6. DIRECTOR / CEO REGISTRATION MECHANICS, AND A WARNING ABOUT THE CEO ROLE

**`LEGAL REQUIREMENT`.** The application for incorporation must state, for each proposed Director and
(if any) the Secretary: **full name (including any previous names), nationality, address, business
occupation (if any), and date of birth**. Directors are appointed by written decision of the
incorporators. The company must maintain a **Register of Directors and Secretaries** under s.90 of
the Companies Regulations, in alphabetical order of names. Changes must be notified to the Registrar
of Companies, deadline reported as **14 days**.

**`PRACTICAL`.** Registration fee **USD 300**; the process takes **about 2 weeks** in practice.
Documents are submitted as **PDF, in English**, via the AIFC self-service portal (specialist sources
also reference `registration@afsa.kz`).

**`RECOMMENDATION` — DROP THE CEO ROLE. This is the most valuable single change available.**

The CEO is optional. Appointing one costs the client four things the package never mentions:

1. An **IIN obligation** that is otherwise contestable for a plain director (row 7 vs row 3).
2. A **mandatory RK employment contract and salary** for the CEO (row 8) — with the payroll, social
   contribution and individual income tax consequences that follow, for a Swiss-resident individual
   who is simultaneously a Swiss GmbH managing director. Cross-border employment and social-security
   exposure that nobody has scoped.
3. The **residency disqualification** at AoA line 367.
4. A **third overlapping title** on top of "Director" and "Authorised Signatory" — feeding the
   title confusion the Lead Reviewer identified at points 5 and 11, where the same man is variously
   Director, Managing Director, CEO and Geschäftsführer across three documents.

A sole Director with the powers already conferred by AoA Art. 18.1 achieves everything the client
needs. If a market-facing "CEO" title is wanted, it can be used commercially without being a
constitutional office.

**`UNCONFIRMED` — a question outside my lane but flagged.** The Brief records the intended business
as sustainability advisory to UN agencies and MDBs, non-regulated, with no AFSA licence sought. The
AFSA gateway is: an entity providing **financial or ancillary services in or from the AIFC** must be
authorised *before* registration. "Ancillary services" is reported to include **consulting**. Whether
this company's advisory work falls inside "ancillary services in or from the AIFC" — and therefore
whether the USD 300 straight-to-registration path is actually available — should be confirmed with
AFSA. It also drives the CIT/VAT question at row 25.

## 7. POWERS OF ATTORNEY — THE ASYMMETRY NOBODY HAS DRAWN

This is the cleanest illustration of the four-way confusion the Brief asked me to unpick, because the
**same document faces two regimes with opposite requirements**:

| Forum the PoA is used before | Apostille? | Notarised RU/KZ translation? | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AFSA / Registrar of Companies** (incorporation, filings) | **No** | **No** — English only | `LEGAL REQUIREMENT (negative)`: *"Documents issued by a foreign country do not require apostil and translation into the Russian/Kazakh language and must be submitted to the AIFC in English."* |
| **RK notaries, State Revenue Committee, NCA (for EDS by representative)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | `LEGAL REQUIREMENT` (RK national law) |
| **Second-tier banks** | **Yes, in practice** | **Yes, in practice** | `Bank/CSP` internal policy: *"All company documents must be notarized and apostilized, as well as translated into Russian and Kazakh."* |

**`FACT`.** Kazakhstan and Switzerland are both parties to the Hague Apostille Convention, so the
**apostille** route applies to a Swiss-executed PoA — **consular legalisation is not required**. The
apostille is affixed in Switzerland by the competent cantonal authority (for a Zug-notarised
instrument, the Canton of Zug State Chancellery).

**`FACT` — a name-transliteration trap.** Where a document does not render the person's full name in
**Cyrillic**, a notarised translation into Kazakh and Russian is required. "Alexander Wiese" /
"Александр Визе" appears inconsistently across this package. The Russian rendering must be **fixed
and identical** across the PoA, the Resolution, the AoA signature block, the IIN application and the
bank forms, or the bank and the notary will treat them as different people. The package currently
uses "Aлександр Визе" — note that in the extracted Resolution the initial "A" is a **Latin A** in a
Cyrillic word in at least two places [Res. lines 14, 47]. That will fail automated matching.
`RECOMMENDATION` (MEDIUM): normalise, and adopt a single agreed Cyrillic spelling before any
government filing.

**`FINDING` — the PoA is scoped for the wrong forum.** The PoA at line 16 lists the AIFC, AFSA, the
Registrar, the State Revenue Committee, second-tier banks and **notaries**. That mixed list is
correct in ambition — but the document is being executed in Zug in English/Russian with, apparently,
no apostille contemplated. It will be **accepted by AFSA** and **rejected by the notary and the bank**.

`RECOMMENDATION` (HIGH): execute **two instruments**, or execute one and apostille it:
- an **English, un-apostilled** PoA for the AFSA incorporation filing; and
- an **apostilled** PoA with **notarised Russian and Kazakh translation** for the notary, the State
  Revenue Committee and the bank.

Executing the second is the only way the PoA's own line 16 reference to notaries and banks means
anything. And note the PoA correctly concedes at line 85 that post-incorporation acts will need a
**separate PoA or corporate resolution issued by the company itself** — which the company cannot
issue until it exists and has a director in office.

**`FINDING` — PoA over-breadth intersects with my lane.** Line 81 lets the attorneys *"increase the
share capital,"* *"execute the Articles of Association,"* and *"approve, execute and file any
amendments to the Articles of Association,"* with **right of substitution** [line 83] and a catch-all
*"any other legal and factual acts."* Read with §5 above, this means **the attorneys could amend the
very Articles the client is relying on**, and could substitute unknown third parties into that power,
for 12 months. Given that the residency and IIN clauses are precisely the clauses that need amending,
this is a power that ought to be exercised **by the shareholder, on advice, not delegated**.
`RECOMMENDATION` (HIGH): strike the share-capital-increase power, strike the AoA-amendment power,
strike the right of substitution, or at minimum make the last exercisable only with prior written
consent. Concurs with Lead Reviewer point 8.

### Must Alexander travel to Astana?

**`UNCONFIRMED` — this is the decisive open question and I cannot close it.**

- **Case for no travel:** the AIFC Expat Centre has *fully transitioned* to online service delivery

**Critically: steps 0–5 do not require Alexander to be in Kazakhstan, do not require an IIN, and do
not require an apostille.** The company can be incorporated remotely from Switzerland. Only banking
and e-government need the personal attendance. **Do not let the CSP present the IIN as a blocker to
