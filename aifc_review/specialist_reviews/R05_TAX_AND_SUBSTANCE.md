## 2. FOUR STATUSES THAT ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION.** The single most expensive error in AIFC planning is conflating these. They are cumulative gates, not synonyms.

| # | Status | Conferred by | What it gives you | What it does **not** give you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AIFC Participant status** | Registration with the AIFC Registrar of Companies | Legal personality under AIFC Acting Law; AIFC courts/IAC; the AIFC currency and employment regimes; the right to *apply* for the rest | **No tax exemption whatsoever.** Registration alone is tax-neutral |
| 2 | **AFSA-regulated / licensed status** | AFSA Licence — either a Regulated Activity (e.g. *Advising on Investments*) or an **Ancillary Service** (Legal / Audit / Accountancy / **Consulting** / Credit Rating) | Permission to carry on that activity in the AIFC by way of business | **Still no tax exemption.** A Consulting ASP licence does **not** exempt income earned from non-qualifying clients |
| 3 | **Kazakhstan tax residency** | Incorporation under KZ law (an AIFC company is a KZ legal entity) **or** place of effective management in KZ | Determines the *base*: a KZ resident is taxed on **worldwide** income; a non-resident only on KZ-source income | Residency is a **burden**, not a benefit. It is the thing the exemption then has to carve into |
| 4 | **Eligibility for the AIFC tax exemption** | Constitutional Statute Art. 6 — **activity-based and counterparty-based**, plus the Substantial Presence Rules | 0% CIT / VAT on the *specific qualifying income stream only*, to 1 Jan 2066 | Does not exempt other income; does not exempt social tax/social payments; does not survive failure of the substance test |

**The client's plan clears gates 1 and (with an application) 2, is automatically caught by gate 3, and — on the intended client base — fails gate 4.**

### 3.2 The exemption — Constitutional Statute Art. 6

**Instrument:** Constitutional Statute of the Republic of Kazakhstan "On the Astana International Financial Centre" No. 438-V ZRK, dated **7 December 2015**. The tax regime is **Article 6**. The horizon commonly cited as "2066" is correct as a *terminal date* — **1 January 2066** — but it is a date, not a blanket.

**Art. 6(3) — financial services limb** (text as extracted; **UNCONFIRMED — I could not open the instrument**):

> "Until 1 January 2066, AIFC Participants, except for the digital asset exchange, are exempt from corporate income tax on income received from providing the following **financial services in the territory of the AIFC**:
> 1) Islamic banking services;
> 2) reinsurance and insurance brokerage services;
> 3) investment management services for assets of investment funds, accounting and safekeeping services for investment funds, as well as services related to issuing, offering, trading, purchase and redemption of securities of investment funds;
> 4) brokerage, dealer or underwriting services;
> 5) any other financial services determined by an AIFC Act."

Subparagraph 5) is exercised through **"The List of financial services provided by the AIFC participants, income from which is exempt from Corporate Income Tax and Value Added Tax"** — a joint act of the AIFC with the Ministry of National Economy and Ministry of Finance, whose **Schedule 1** contains the operative enumeration. **UNCONFIRMED — INPUT MISSING: I could not retrieve Schedule 1 in full.** Every secondary description of Schedule 1 I could obtain describes *financial* services (banking, insurance, funds, securities, custody, payments, Islamic finance). **No source describes generic non-financial consultancy as being in Schedule 1.**

**Art. 6 — ancillary services limb** (paragraph number **UNCONFIRMED**; substance corroborated by five independent sources):

> Until 1 January 2066, AIFC Participants are exempt from corporate income tax on income received from **legal, audit, accounting and consulting services provided to AIFC Bodies and to AIFC Participants that provide financial services**.

The AIFC's own rules give an **exhaustive** list of Ancillary Services: (1) legal; (2) audit; (3) accountancy; (4) provision of expert knowledge or advice on a specific topic (consulting); (5) credit rating/scoring. `LEGAL REQUIREMENT` (per AFSA Ancillary Services framework, s.24 AIFC Financial Services Framework Regulations — section number UNCONFIRMED).

**The two limbs have different tests.** Limb 1 is a **service-type** test (is it one of the listed financial services, performed in the AIFC?). Limb 2 is a **counterparty** test (is the recipient an AIFC Body, or an AIFC Participant that provides financial services?). Consultancy is **only** reachable through limb 2, and limb 2 is a closed circle: **the exemption is for consultants serving the AIFC's own financial ecosystem.**

## 4. THE DECISIVE QUESTION — DOES SUSTAINABILITY ADVISORY TO UN AGENCIES AND MDBs QUALIFY?

### **Answer: No. Not on the facts given. This is the centre of gravity of the whole file.**

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION.** Severity: **CRITICAL.**

Run the intended business through both limbs.

**Limb 1 — financial services (Art. 6(3)).**
Sustainability advisory is not Islamic banking, not reinsurance or insurance broking, not fund management/administration/custody, not brokerage/dealing/underwriting. It could only enter through subparagraph 5) — the Schedule 1 List — and nothing in any available description of that List reaches non-financial advisory. **Fails.**

**Limb 2 — ancillary services (consulting).**
The service *type* is right: "provision of expert knowledge or advice on a specific topic" plainly covers sustainability advisory. **But the counterparty test fails.** The exemption reaches consulting supplied **to AIFC Bodies and to AIFC Participants providing financial services**. UN agencies (UNDP, UNEP, UNIDO, FAO, UNOPS…) and MDBs (EBRD, ADB, AIIB, IsDB, World Bank Group, EIB) are, save in a narrow case addressed below, **neither**. They are non-AIFC, and in most cases non-Kazakhstan, international organisations.

The point is not marginal or arguable at the edges — it is the express architecture of the provision. The AIFC did not create a general offshore consultancy exemption; it created a support-services exemption **for the professional firms that service its financial industry**. A consultancy whose entire client base sits outside that circle is, for CIT purposes, an ordinary Kazakhstan company.

**Corroboration.** Every source I could reach states the limitation in substantially identical terms, including that *"this exemption is limited to services provided to AIFC entities — it does not extend to services provided to clients outside the AIFC"*, and that non-qualifying income *"would be subject to the standard 20% corporate income tax rate."*

**Consequence — separate accounting.** The **Regulations on keeping separate accounting by AIFC participants for corporate income tax** require a participant to segregate exempt income (and the deductions allocable to it) from taxable income. A participant with **no** exempt stream does not escape this; it simply reports 100% taxable. `LEGAL REQUIREMENT` (UNCONFIRMED as to citation).

### 4.1 The one genuine, narrow exception worth pricing

**FINDING TYPE: RECOMMENDATION.** Some MDBs and development-finance institutions **do** hold AIFC Participant status and provide financial services (the AIFC actively recruits them; the AIFC Green Finance Centre operates in exactly that ecosystem). **To the extent — and only to the extent — that a specific client is an AIFC Body or an AIFC Participant that provides financial services, fees from that client are capable of falling inside limb 2.**

This is a real planning point, but it must be stress-tested honestly:
- it applies **client by client**, verified against the AIFC public register at the time of contracting;
- it will be a **minority** of a UN/MDB-facing practice, not the base case;
- it requires **separate accounting** to isolate;
- and it is **still subject to the Substantial Presence Rules** (§10), which minimal-substance plans fail.

Do not build a business case on it. Treat it as upside on an otherwise fully taxable company.

### 4.2 A blunt commercial observation

**FINDING TYPE: RECOMMENDATION.** If tax is genuinely a primary motive, note that on these facts the AIFC company adds a **20% CIT layer that the Swiss parent alone would not suffer on the same fees**, plus KZ payroll charges, plus AIFC registration and ASP licence fees (Ancillary Services application fee **USD 2,800**, plus annual fees), plus audit, plus AIFC substance cost, plus Substantial Presence reporting. On the tax metric alone, **doing the work from Zug is cheaper than doing it from Astana.** That should be put to the client in writing before incorporation, so that the decision to proceed is made on the market-access rationale with eyes open.

## 5. THE ARTICLES' OBJECTS CLAUSE MAKES THIS WORSE, NOT BETTER

**Document evidence:** AoA line 114 (English) / line 116 (Russian).

> "The principal activities of the Company are the provision of **advisory services in the fields of finance**, corporate governance and business development, as well as the provision of other advisory and support services to legal entities and individuals **both within and outside** the Astana International Financial Centre (AIFC)."

**Three distinct problems. Severity: HIGH.**

**(a) It does not help the tax position — it *documents* the failure.** The words "**both within and outside** the AIFC" are a written admission, in the company's own constitution, that its client base extends beyond the AIFC perimeter. In a CIT audit or a Substantial Presence review this is the first sentence the assessor will quote. `LEGAL INTERPRETATION`

**(b) "Advisory services in the fields of finance" creates AFSA regulatory-perimeter exposure while delivering no tax benefit.** *Advising on Investments* and *Advising on a Credit Facility* are **Regulated Activities** requiring an AFSA licence (Advising on Investments carries a Base Capital Requirement of **USD 10,000** — `UNCONFIRMED`). An objects clause announcing "advisory in the field of finance" invites AFSA and any bank's compliance function to ask whether a licence is required. Carrying on a Regulated Activity without a licence exposes the firm to fines, public enforcement and suspension, and **agreements written outside the permitted perimeter may be unenforceable**, meaning fees may be irrecoverable. The clause therefore imports the *regulatory* downside of financial advisory with **none** of the tax upside. `LEGAL INTERPRETATION` — **this is the worst of both worlds and should be fixed before filing.**

**(c) It does not describe the actual business.** "Sustainability advisory" appears nowhere in the objects. Three separate downstream processes read this clause as the company's self-description: AFSA authorisation, bank account KYC (the PoA at lines 39/60 has the attorneys submitting "information regarding the company's business activities"), and the **economic study** required under the Substantial Presence Rules. A constitution that misdescribes the business will be contradicted by the business plan submitted alongside it.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Redraft line 114 to state the real activity and drop the financial framing — e.g. *"the provision of consultancy services in the field of sustainability, climate and environmental, social and governance advisory to international organisations, development finance institutions, public bodies and corporates"* — and take a written AFSA perimeter view before filing. Consider whether the "within and outside the AIFC" formulation needs to be there at all; it is not required by the Companies Regulations and it volunteers an adverse fact.

### 8.2 Swiss side — participation exemption on the inbound dividend

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION.** Wiese Advisory GmbH holding **100%** of the AIFC company comfortably exceeds the Swiss qualifying-participation thresholds (**≥10% of capital, or fair market value ≥CHF 1,000,000**). ## 10. SUBSTANCE — THE SUBSTANTIAL PRESENCE RULES

**Instrument:** *Rules on the Substantial Presence of the AIFC Participants Applying Tax Exemptions for the payment of CIT, VAT*, adopted by AFSA in coordination with the Ministry of Finance, **in force from 1 January 2022**, introduced as part of Kazakhstan's OECD BEPS / harmful-tax-practices commitments (the AIFC regime was assessed by the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices in 2022). A joint AFSA / State Revenue Committee **Guidance** supplements them. `UNCONFIRMED as to text.`

**Three mandatory requirements:**

| Requirement | Test |
|---|---|
| **Core Income Generating Activity (CIGA)** | The CIGA — being the services set out in the Constitutional Statute and/or the List of Financial Services — must be **carried on in the territory of the Centre** |
| **Adequate operating expenditure** | Operating expenses incurred must be **commensurate** with the CIGA performed |
| **Adequate qualified employees** | The number of **qualified full-time** employees must be **commensurate** with the CIGA performed |

Participants must submit to the local state revenue authority an **economic study** of the project justifying costs and headcount.

**Application to this client. Severity: CRITICAL.**

1. **The rules only matter if you have exempt income.** On §4's conclusion, the company has none — so paradoxically the Substantial Presence Rules are *not* the binding constraint. Being fully taxable, the company does not need to pass them. **This is not good news; it is the shape of the failure.**
2. **If** the client pursues the narrow AIFC-participant-client stream (§4.1), or if the AoA framing later leads AFSA/SRC to test the position, then **"minimal local substance planned"** fails all three limbs: CIGA is performed in Zug, not in the AIFC; there is no local operating expenditure of substance; and there are no qualified full-time employees.
3. A company "routing income through an AIFC shell without real operations" is expressly identified in commentary as facing **re-assessment at mainland rates (20% CIT, 16% VAT)** — with interest and penalties, retrospectively.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Whatever the tax conclusion, **the substance question must be answered before incorporation, not after** — because it also drives (a) POEM (§12), (b) bank account opening, which is the practical gating item in the PoA, and (c) the credibility of the AIFC presence to UN/MDB counterparties, who conduct their own due diligence on vendors.

## 12. PLACE OF EFFECTIVE MANAGEMENT AND TAX RESIDENCY — **THE SHARPEST STRUCTURAL RISK**

**FINDING TYPE: LEGAL INTERPRETATION.** Severity: **CRITICAL.**

The facts: sole director and CEO is Swiss-resident, sits in Zug, holds no IIN; the sole shareholder is in Zug; minimal local substance is planned; day-to-day control is exercised from Switzerland. There are **two mirror-image risks**, and the mitigations for each pull in opposite directions.

### 12.1 Risk A — the AIFC company is treated as **Swiss** tax resident

Under **Art. 50 DBG** (and the parallel cantonal/StHG rule), a legal entity with its **effective management** (*tatsächliche Verwaltung*) in Switzerland is **resident in Switzerland** and taxable there on its worldwide income — **regardless of where it is incorporated**. Swiss doctrine locates residence where control is actually exercised, and Swiss authorities require real substance: a registered address or a nominee director does not establish or displace residence.

The Swiss Federal Supreme Court has applied this squarely: where a holding company's effective management was exercised **from the directors' residences**, the statutory seat elsewhere was treated as **purely formal** and the company was fully taxable where the directors sat.

**On these facts that is close to the paradigm case.** One director, resident in Zug, making all decisions in Zug, for a company with no local management in Astana. **The realistic outcome is a Swiss claim that the AIFC company is Swiss tax resident**, with:
- Swiss CIT on its worldwide profit;
- a **dual residence** conflict with Kazakhstan (which taxes it as a KZ-incorporated entity), resolved — if at all — under the DTT tie-breaker, which for the KZ–CH treaty is likely place of effective management or a mutual agreement procedure. **The tie-breaker would point to Switzerland**, which defeats the entire point of the structure;
- loss of the AIFC dividend exemption and any AIFC tax feature as a practical matter;
- Swiss late-filing and penalty exposure for the years the company filed nowhere in Switzerland.

The AoA's own Art. 24.9 (line 462) is relevant evidence here: a directors' meeting *"shall be deemed to take place where the largest group of participants is assembled, failing which… where the chairperson is physically located."* **With a sole director in Zug, the company's own constitution deems its board meetings to occur in Zug.** That is a document the Swiss authorities would be entitled to read.

### 12.2 Risk B — Wiese Advisory GmbH has a **Kazakhstan permanent establishment**

The mirror risk. Kazakhstan's domestic PE definition is **broader than its treaty definitions**, and expressly covers:
- a fixed place of business;
- **services rendered through personnel** for more than **183 days in any consecutive 12-month period** on the same or connected projects (service PE);
- a **dependent agent** habitually concluding contracts;
- participation in joint activity.

If the GmbH's personnel (in practice, Wiese) spend time in Astana delivering or supervising, or if the AIFC company habitually acts for the GmbH, the GmbH itself may register a KZ PE — attracting **CIT on PE profits plus branch profits tax** and a registration obligation within **30 days** of commencing activity.

### 12.3 The tension, stated plainly

**Fixing Risk A requires putting real management in Astana. Doing that increases Risk B and costs money — which is the very thing "minimal local substance" was meant to avoid.** There is no free position. The client must choose:

| Option | Tax residence | Substance cost | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(i) Real Astana management** — appoint a KZ-resident CEO as the AoA already requires (Art. 18.2), board meets in Astana, local office and staff | KZ | Material | The only version that is internally coherent with the drafted Articles. Also the only version that could ever pass Substantial Presence. Wiese steps back to Director/shareholder representative, not CEO |
| **(ii) Keep Zug management, accept the consequences** | Likely CH (Art. 50 DBG) | Minimal | Then ask honestly what the KZ company is *for*. Answer: market access and credibility — which may be enough, but must be priced with a Swiss tax charge and a documented dual-residence position |

## 13. TRANSFER PRICING AND THE INTERCOMPANY SERVICE AGREEMENT

**Instrument:** Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan **No. 67-IV "On Transfer Pricing" dated 5 July 2008**, as amended (notably by the amending law of 25 March 2024 and by **Law No. 215-VIII of 18 July 2025**). `FACT` (citation UNCONFIRMED as to current consolidated text).

**Why this bites hard here. Severity: HIGH.**

1. **Kazakhstan TP applies to ALL cross-border transactions — not only related-party ones.** This is unusual and catches practitioners out. Every invoice between the GmbH and the AIFC company, and arguably every cross-border client invoice, is within scope.
2. **Deviation from arm's length can be recharacterised as a constructive dividend**, attracting withholding tax — on top of the primary adjustment.
3. **Documentation:** parties must maintain records justifying the transaction price and produce them **within 90 days** of a request. Kazakhstan operates **three-tier documentation** (Master File, Local File, CbCR; CbCR threshold **EUR 750m**, converted at the National Bank rate from 2026 — not reached here, but Local File obligations can arise at far lower thresholds).
4. **Intragroup services, royalties and intercompany interest are expressly flagged risk areas** by the Kazakhstan authorities.

**Because the AIFC company is fully taxable (§4), transfer pricing becomes the *primary* determinant of how much Kazakhstan tax is actually paid.** Where profit sits between Zug and Astana is now a live 20% question, not an academic one. Conversely — and this cuts against the client — an aggressive attempt to strip profit out of Astana to Zug is precisely what the TP Law and the constructive-dividend rule are built to stop.

**PRACTICAL REQUIREMENT — before any invoicing:**
- A written **intercompany services agreement** between Wiese Advisory GmbH and Wiese Advisory Eurasia Ltd., executed on both sides by properly authorised signatories (note the signature-capacity defects at AoA line 602 and Res. line 43 — **the same defect will infect the ICA if not fixed**);
- a documented **functional analysis**: who performs which functions, who bears which risks, who owns the intangibles (methodology, client relationships, brand). On the current facts the GmbH performs nearly all functions and owns all intangibles — which points to a **low routine return** in Kazakhstan (cost-plus on a narrow cost base), not to profit shifting *into* Kazakhstan;
- a **benchmarked** remuneration policy (cost-plus mark-up for support services; a defensible split for any front-facing delivery);
- clarity on **which entity contracts with the UN agency or MDB**. This is the single most consequential commercial decision in the file: UN/MDB procurement rules, vendor registration, and their own withholding and immunity positions may dictate the answer independently of tax. Decide it before the Astana entity is marketed to clients;
- an **intercompany loan agreement** if the GmbH funds the subsidiary, with an arm's-length rate, plus a thin-capitalisation check.

## 14. GERMANY — FLAGGED, NOT ANALYSED

**FINDING TYPE: RECOMMENDATION.** Alexander Wiese is a **German national resident in Switzerland**. On the stated facts German tax should not currently apply — but German nationality plus a low-taxed foreign entity plus proximity to Germany is a combination that warrants a German opinion rather than an assumption. **I flag, and do not analyse, the following. German counsel required.**

1. **CFC / Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung (§§ 7–14 AStG).** German CFC taxation applies to persons with **unlimited German tax liability**. The **low-tax threshold was reduced to 15% with effect from 1 January 2024** (aligned to Pillar Two), and applies to §7 control-based attribution, §13 investment-income attribution, and extended attribution under the Tax Haven Defence Act. **If Mr Wiese is, becomes, or is later found to be German tax resident, an AIFC company taxed at 0% on any income stream is squarely in low-tax territory.** Whether the income is "passive" turns on the §8(1) AStG active-income catalogue — services rendered to a related party can be tainted, and an "established business operation" (*eingerichteter Geschäftsbetrieb*) is required. Note the irony: **the §4 conclusion that the company is fully taxable at 20% is the fact that keeps it above the 15% CFC threshold.** A *successful* tax exemption would *create* German CFC risk. This must be modelled jointly, not in isolation.
2. **German tax residence risk (§§ 8, 9 AO).** A dwelling available in Germany, or habitual abode, can restore unlimited liability irrespective of Swiss registration. Confirm the position affirmatively; do not infer it from Swiss residence alone.
3. **Extended limited tax liability (§ 2 AStG)** and the **Germany–Switzerland DTT Art. 4** overriding-taxation rules (*überdachende Besteuerung*) for Germans resident in Switzerland — specialist, fact-sensitive, and easy to trip.
4. **Exit tax (§ 6 AStG)** — relevant only if the move to Switzerland is recent or if shares were transferred around the move. Confirm the historic position is closed.
5. **Personal taxation of director's fees / salary** paid by a Kazakhstan company to a Swiss-resident German national — the KZ–CH and DE–CH treaties interact.

**What German advice is needed:** a short written confirmation of (a) current residence status and its stability, (b) whether §§7–14 AStG could apply on any foreseeable change, (c) §2 AStG and DTT Art. 4 exposure, and (d) the historic §6 AStG position. This is a scoped one-off, not an ongoing engagement.

## 17. VERDICT ON THE CLIENT'S TAX MOTIVE

**Stated plainly, as instructed:**

**The tax motive is unlikely to be satisfied on the current facts.** The AIFC exemption is not an offshore regime and does not attach to AIFC registration. It attaches to a defined set of **financial services** performed in the Centre, and to a defined set of **support services supplied into the Centre's financial ecosystem**. Sustainability advisory sold to UN agencies and MDBs outside Kazakhstan is neither. The realistic tax outcome is **20% Kazakhstan corporate income tax on worldwide profit**, employer social charges of roughly 22.5% on any local payroll, and VAT at 16% on any in-scope supplies — that is, the ordinary Kazakhstan regime, reached via a more expensive and more heavily supervised route than a mainland LLP.

Two qualifications, both honest:

- **There is one exemption of real value that does survive**: the AIFC **dividend** exemption on distributions to the shareholder, which appears not to depend on the trading income being exempt. If confirmed, it means profits can be taxed once in Kazakhstan at 20% and then repatriated to Zug without Kazakhstan withholding, where the Swiss participation deduction should relieve them again. That is a **respectable** — not spectacular — outcome, and it is materially better than the alternative of 20% plus 5–15% withholding. **Verify this before anything else.**
- **The non-tax case is intact.** AIFC registration gives a common-law platform, English-language dispute resolution, an AFSA-authorised consultancy licence recognisable to UN and MDB procurement functions, proximity to the Green Finance Centre's ICMA/CBI-accredited green-finance ecosystem, and a credible Central Asian footprint for regional mandates. **If the client would still incorporate knowing the tax answer is "20%", the structure is sound. If not, it should not proceed.** That question should be put to him in writing, and his answer recorded on the file.

The one outcome that must be avoided is incorporating on the tax assumption, discovering it in year two during a Substantial Presence review or a Swiss residence enquiry, and facing retrospective assessment in **two** jurisdictions at once.
